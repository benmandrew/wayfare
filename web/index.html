Most lines are a road segment — a stretch of road between two junctions. Thickness shows how many distinct bus and coach services use it, so trunk corridors stand out from the tail.

Colour by chooses what the ramp means. Services matches the thickness: how many services run here at all. Journeys a day counts departures instead, averaged over the whole week and drawn on a logarithmic scale, so one route every ten minutes reads as busy where a service count calls it one road out of one. Grey means the archive under you carries no departure count at that zoom.

Trams, metros, trains and ferries have no road under them, so they are drawn from the operator's own trace and keep a colour of their own, listed in the key below. Under Services that colour is flat, because a line on its own track is always one service. Under Journeys a day each mode is shaded along its own colour on the same scale as the bar above, so a quiet ferry and a busy one are the same red at different depths. Each line is one route's trace, so where two share a track they are two lines, not one busier one.

Hover a line for its service list. Filtering matches whole service numbers, so 4 will not match 42.

Zoomed out, the map thins the densest areas to keep tiles small, so a quiet-looking city is not necessarily a city without services. Zoom in for the complete picture.
